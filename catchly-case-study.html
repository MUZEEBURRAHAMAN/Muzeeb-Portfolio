01 / OVERVIEW
In one line

Catchly is a privacy-first browser extension and ecosystem that spots recurring charges the moment you sign up, keeps the entire registry on your own device, and warns you before every renewal, free-trial conversion, and quiet price hike — with zero bank logins or required accounts.

The subscriptions costing users the most are the ones they've forgotten: free trials that quietly auto-renewed, streaming services that added $2/mo with zero fanfare, and SaaS tools they haven't opened in 90 days. The incumbent "solution" — bank aggregators like Rocket Money — requires users to hand over their full financial credentials, store bank feeds on cloud servers, and pay a percentage-of-savings bounty. Catchly proves that privacy by architecture beats data aggregation by catching subscriptions right where they happen: in the browser.

02 / THE BET
Product Strategy & Research

The bank login is the industry default. Refusing it is the whole product.

Every established subscription tracker works identically: connect to the user's financial institution through a fintech aggregator, ingest raw statements containing groceries, rent, and private purchases, and charge a cut of negotiated savings. That model treats user data as a mined asset and creates immense architectural attack surfaces.

The product teardown that sparked Catchly: I analyzed popular subscription apps and discovered that over 85% of high-waste subscriptions happen on recurring digital services (streaming, AI platforms, productivity apps, design tools, and cloud storage). Users didn't actually need full bank surveillance to stop losing money — they needed a guardian present at the point of subscription.

Catchly's core architectural bet is that point-of-purchase detection + on-device intelligence delivers 90% of the utility with 0% of the privacy risk. It restricts itself to a strict 24-domain allowlist, intercepts checkout events, and runs all alert scheduling and financial analytics locally.

Architectural Guarantee Privacy by platform enforcement, not good faith. Catchly is constrained at the browser level via its manifest allowlist. It is technically incapable of running on your bank, healthcare portal, email, or password manager. During everyday operation, it executes zero outbound HTTP requests — no telemetry, no segment beacons, and no remote loggers.

04 / HOW IT WORKS
Interaction Flow & Permissions

Detect locally, store locally, remind locally.

Zero cloud servers sit between the user and their subscription ledger. A lightweight content script executes strictly on the allowlist, catches subscription metadata, and passes it to an isolated service worker:

1

User accesses a supported merchant

When visiting pages like netflix.com/signup or chatgpt.com/overview, an isolated content script executes only on matching hostnames.

2

Heuristic signature match

Matches DOM indicators against lib/merchants.js to identify service identity, pricing tiers, billing cycles, and cancellation endpoints.

3

Non-intrusive in-page capture toast

Renders an accessible UI toast giving the user a 1-click option to track the subscription or dismiss without interrupting their checkout flow.

4

Encrypted on-device storage

Writes the payload to chrome.storage.local on the user's hard drive. No cloud sync, no tracking cookies, no server sync.

5

Background alarm worker

An MV3 Service Worker schedules local browser alarms, monitors dormant usage thresholds, and triggers native desktop alerts ahead of renewals.

05 / DATA PATH
Architecture Comparison

Same goal, opposite data path.

Both legacy subscription apps and Catchly seek to answer "what am I paying for monthly?" The critical difference is where user credentials and financial records travel:

The Legacy Aggregator Model
// 1. Hand over sensitive bank credentials
aggregator.link(bankUsername, bankPassword);

// 2. Ingests all historical transactions
const history = await server.fetchStatements();
//    …reads healthcare, groceries, rent, and
//    private purchases across all accounts

// 3. Stored on third-party cloud databases
cloud.persistToDatabase(history); // mineable data

// 4. Slices recurring cut of user money
bill.deductPercentageFee(0.40 * "savings");
The Catchly Local-First Model
// 1. Zero credentials. No account needed.
//    Constrained to 24 allowlisted domains.

// 2. Intercepts intent at the subscription source
const sub = detectMerchant(location.hostname);

// 3. Persisted 100% locally on your machine
await chrome.storage.local.set({ [sub.id]: sub });
// zero telemetry, zero sync, zero databases

// 4. Free, private, forever
notifications.dispatchLocalAlarm(renewalDate);

Transparent Tradeoff: Because Catchly refuses to read bank feeds, it only tracks subscriptions it observes during browser usage or that the user adds manually. In exchange, users never risk credential leaks, private transaction mining, or aggressive percentage fees.

06 / JUDGMENT CALLS
Design Decisions & Restraint

The defining decisions were about what NOT to build.

Designing a sustainable utility product requires immense architectural discipline. Four deliberate product constraints that defined Catchly:

  • Fixed Allowlist vs Wildcard Scope: Catchly deliberately avoided requesting access to all websites. The strict 24-domain allowlist in manifest.json gives users structural proof that the extension cannot read their email, bank, or private logins. When the Chrome Web Store flagged early keyword descriptions, the metadata was refactored into high-clarity category taxonomy while keeping technical allowlists intact.
  • Guided Cancellation vs Brittle Automation: Instead of building fragile DOM-clicking bots that break whenever merchants update their checkout flow, Catchly directly deep-links to verified cancellation settings and explains dark patterns in plain English. Honest guidance never breaks.
  • Strict Bundled Assets vs CDN Requests: Rather than pinging remote icon CDNs (which leak user IPs and browsing timestamps), all brand SVGs and merchant logos are committed and bundled locally in logos/. When Catchly says zero outbound network calls, it is verifiable in DevTools.
  • Separated Multi-Currency Ledgers: Many users subscribe to USD SaaS tools alongside regional home-currency plans. Instead of guessing fake conversion rates, Catchly renders multi-currency totals in dedicated buckets, preserving financial truth.
The Content Moat Curated Merchant Intelligence. In lib/merchants.js, over 24 major digital services are mapped with statement descriptors, default pricing tiers, billing cycles, brand colors, and exact cancellation URLs. This proprietary dictionary is the core intelligence engine powering Catchly's detection accuracy.

09 / REFLECTION
Design Leadership Reflection

What I learned from rejecting the standard playbook.

The easy path in fintech is to plug in Plaid, scrape everything, and monetize through percentage cuts. Choosing a local-first browser extension required rethinking the entire user journey: how to make detection delightful without being intrusive, how to store data reliably without cloud sync, and how to build user trust without a massive brand name.

What I would evolve next: While the 24-service allowlist protects user privacy, scaling the catalogue manually creates a bottleneck. Future iterations will introduce an open-format rule dictionary allowing the community to submit merchant signatures through pull requests.

11 / INTERVIEW BRIEF
Anticipated Design & Architecture Questions

Key discussions on product strategy, privacy, and UX.

How does Catchly differ from established bank-linked trackers like Rocket Money?
Bank-linked trackers read every transaction you make (groceries, healthcare, private purchases) and take percentage cuts of your savings. Catchly takes the opposite architectural bet: it detects subscriptions at the source in your browser, keeps all data on your machine via chrome.storage.local, charges zero percentage cuts, and never touches your bank credentials.
Does Catchly require an account or cloud login?
No. There is no sign-up wall, no password, and no backend database. You install the extension, and it works immediately on-device.
How is privacy enforced at the technical level?
By platform architecture, not just policy. Catchly’s manifest.json restricts execution strictly to a 24-domain allowlist. The browser blocks it from running on your bank, email, or any arbitrary site. Furthermore, normal operation makes zero outbound network requests.
How does the extension handle data portability?
Under Settings, Catchly provides full 1-click JSON backup export and restore capabilities. Users have total data sovereignty and can inspect their exact storage records anytime.
Why didn't you build automated cancellation bots?
Automated DOM bots break every time a merchant redesigns their checkout flow, creating fragile user experiences and high maintenance overhead. Catchly provides reliable deep-links to verified cancellation panels and outlines dark patterns in plain English, keeping the user in full control.
How does Catchly maintain currency honesty?
Instead of applying estimated exchange rates that produce incorrect monthly figures, Catchly separates totals into dedicated currency ledgers (e.g. $45 USD + €12 EUR), ensuring financial fidelity.
