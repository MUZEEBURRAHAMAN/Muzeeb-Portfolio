Where every conversation is part of a case, and every record carries the weight of privilege.

CaseNotes is an AI meeting notetaker built specifically for lawyers who cannot treat sensitive client consultations like sales calls.

Designed around privilege, not just transcription. Every client conversation may become part of a case file, subject to discovery, privilege review, and bar association conduct rules. CaseNotes treats "can this record exist, and who can see it" as a core design constraint, not a buried settings toggle.

Built as matter memory, not an isolated meeting log. Summaries attach directly to active cases. Decisions, court deadlines, and privilege flags connect across the matter's lifecycle, routing straight to assignees without paralegals manually re-entering notes into practice management software.

The Opening Insight In 9 of the 12 client meetings shadowed during discovery, associates paused or stopped recordings the moment discussions turned toward settlement numbers or strategy, and scrambled afterward to reconstruct that part from memory. That is a structural flaw in general-purpose AI tools.

Every AI notetaker treats a meeting as a meeting. In law, a meeting is evidence.

Higher-accuracy transcription models do not solve the core failure modes of legal note-taking:

Scattered Matter Records

  • Case notes live scattered across practice management, document storage, calendars, and external notetakers.
  • Paralegals re-type identical notes, billing logs, and task lists across multiple tools after every call.
  • Critical details get lost or mistyped during manual copy-pasting between systems.

Privilege Waiver Risk

  • In United States v. Heppner, courts established that consumer AI platforms lack the fiduciary relationship required to preserve attorney-client privilege.
  • Passing client strategy through generic notetakers can waive privilege during discovery.
  • Lawyers are forced to choose between efficiency and malpractice exposure.

Zero Cross-Meeting Memory

  • General tools treat each meeting as an isolated file with no awareness of the overarching matter.
  • Nothing connects a settlement discussion in March to deposition preparation in October.
  • Case context is lost when associates rotate off files or leave the firm.
Legal Precedent United States v. Heppner, as highlighted by Mayer Brown: uncontrolled consumer AI transcription creates discoverable liabilities in litigation.

Auditing 12 tools: 3 out of 4 failure modes were legal risks, not UX complaints

General tools compete on raw transcription speed and automated email summaries. In legal practice, that ignores confidentiality requirements, privilege logs, and ethical walls.

Fireflies.ai

  • No privilege controls: every word captured is discoverable by default in litigation.
  • Intrusive meeting bot creates social friction and awkward moments on client calls.
  • No matter linkage: summaries live siloed in Fireflies rather than the active case file.
  • Consumer-grade data handling not aligned with bar rules of professional conduct.

Otter.ai

  • Same consumer-AI privilege exposure, with no fiduciary relationship under federal rules.
  • Past class-action lawsuits over recording consent practices erode client trust.
  • Arbitrary meeting length caps right at deposition prep durations.
  • Zero practice management or document management integration.

Fathom

  • Bot presence remains visible on calls despite marketing claims, creating exposure.
  • Lacks SOC 2 / ISO compliance configurations needed for legal-grade confidentiality.
  • Steep pricing jump from free to paid with no tier scaled for boutique law firms.
  • No concept of matters, case files, or privilege tiers across client interactions.

Lua Legal-Specific Competitor

  • Direct legal competitor with SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, and off-the-record controls.
  • Strong on real-time assistance and administrative time entries for billing.
  • Thinner on cross-matter memory: treats meetings as standalone records rather than connected timeline nodes.
  • Lacks role-tuned views: partners, associates, paralegals, and clients all receive the same interface.
A meeting that needs a 14,000-word transcript to be useful wasn't a meeting. In a law firm, it's also a discovery liability waiting to happen.

Shadowing lawyers before drawing a screen: discovery on real legal constraints

I observed 12 live client meetings and case conferences across four small-to-midsize firms, conducted 7 follow-up interviews with partners, associates, and paralegals, and audited published AI notetaker guidance from Mayer Brown, Cahill, and the American Bar Association (ABA).

1. Ad-Hoc Pauses

Associates used personal, undocumented rules to pause recorders during settlement talks, creating firm-wide inconsistency.

2. Paralegal Tax

Paralegals spent hours re-typing identical meeting notes into practice management software, document drives, and billing logs.

3. Meeting Boundaries

Lawyers treat internal team huddles and privileged client sessions differently, but standard tools handle both identically.

The Design Thesis

Principle 1: The lawyer controls whether a record exists in real time. A one-tap shortcut pauses capture mid-sentence and marks the segment privileged, going off the record without breaking conversational flow.

Principle 2: Every output is linked to a matter. Summaries attach directly to the case record, carry privilege tags, and stay restricted behind firm ethical walls.

1. Before: Tag the matter, inherit its defaults

CaseNotes links with firm calendars and practice management software. Before a meeting begins, it checks the matter assignment and applies inherited privilege defaults (litigation calls default to privileged and restricted; firm administrative meetings default to open).

Setup Flow
Calendar Integration & Matter-Tagging Sequence

Calendar matching, case selection, and automatic privilege profile inheritance.

2. During: Minimal ambient UI & one-keystroke control

During calls, CaseNotes stays ambient: no bot avatars or scrolling transcript text. A discreet indicator shows recording status, and a single shortcut pauses capture for sensitive discussions.

In-Meeting UI
Ambient Recording & Privilege Pause Toast

Minimalist status indicator alongside a 'Recording paused (marked privileged)' notification.

3. After: Structured intelligence, filed where it belongs

Post-meeting summaries file directly to the case: context, decisions, assignees, deadlines, and segment-by-segment privilege tags. Raw transcripts remain in a secondary drawer to minimize discovery exposure.

Hero Surface
Post-Meeting Structured Summary View

Matter header, decisions, action items, segment-by-segment privilege toggles, and transcript drawer.

The transcript is evidence. The summary is intelligence. In a law firm, shipping the transcript by default isn't just a UX miss; it creates a wider surface for a privilege fight.

Key Design Decisions

Systems Over Screens

Designed an interconnected matter-linked architecture rather than generic transcription screens, supporting multi-role legal workflows.

Compliance-Led Design

Integrated ethical walls, privilege logs, and discovery defensibility into the core product rather than treating compliance as an afterthought.

Role-Specific Scoping

Created structural boundaries for four distinct user tiers, ensuring sensitive work product is never exposed to unauthorized recipients.

Most AI products treat compliance as a checkbox after design is done. In law, compliance is a design input from the first screen. Get the wrong default here and the product doesn't have a rough edge; it has a liability.
