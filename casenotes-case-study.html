01The problem

In law, a meeting is evidence.

Imagine you're an associate on a call with a client. The client starts talking about settlement numbers.

You reach over and pause the AI notetaker. Courts have said consumer AI tools don't preserve attorney-client privilege, so the recording could end up in discovery. Now there's a gap in the notes, and you rebuild it from memory.

After the call, a paralegal re-types the same notes into three different tools. And nothing connects this call to the deposition prep in October.

02What CaseNotes does

Capture the case, protect the privilege.

Generic notetakers treat every meeting the same. CaseNotes is built around three things a law firm needs:

01

Go off the record in one keystroke

The lawyer decides, in the moment, whether a record exists at all.

02

File every note to its matter

Summaries attach to the case, carry privilege tags, and stay behind ethical walls.

03

Remember the case, not the meeting

Decisions and deadlines connect across months of client and counsel sessions.

03What I saw

I sat in on 12 client meetings before drawing a screen.

I observed 12 live client meetings and case conferences across four small and midsize firms, then ran 7 follow-up interviews with partners, associates, and paralegals.

In 9 of the 12 meetings, associates paused or stopped the recording when the talk turned to settlement or strategy.

Finding 1

Pauses had no rules

Each associate used a personal habit to decide when to stop recording, so the firm's records were inconsistent.

Finding 2

Paralegals paid the price

They spent hours re-typing the same notes into practice management software, document drives, and billing logs.

Finding 3

Not every meeting is equal

Lawyers treat an internal huddle and a privileged client session differently. Their tools didn't.

06Privilege

How do I go off the record?

One keystroke, ⌘+P on desktop or one tap on mobile, pauses capture mid-sentence and marks that segment privileged.

The screen switches to a high-contrast state so everyone can see nothing is being recorded. If opposing counsel walks in, one tap is enough.

07AI you can check

Can I trust what the AI wrote?

CaseNotes only summarizes what someone said. If a term wasn't discussed, it says so instead of guessing.

Every point links to the exact moment in the audio, so a lawyer can verify it in seconds. Client data never trains the AI models.

08Roles

Who sees what?

The same meeting shows up differently for each person, based on what their job needs and what the ethics rules allow.

RoleWhat they see from the same meeting
PartnerDecisions, risk, billable time, and staffing.
AssociateResearch tasks, drafting deadlines, and cited sources.
ParalegalFiling dates, document requests, and docket items.
ClientA plain-language digest. Privileged notes are removed before display.

09Integrations

Where do the notes go?

Into the tools the firm already uses. Summaries, deadlines, and time entries push to Clio, PracticePanther, or MyCase. Memos go to NetDocuments or iManage with the firm's existing permissions.

Inside Omnis, notes feed the CRM, MedChron timelines, and LitDraft documents.

10Design decisions

Four calls that shaped CaseNotes.

Decision 1

Why does the lawyer control recording?

Associates were already pausing recorders by habit. Making it one keystroke, and visible to everyone, turned a private workaround into a firm-wide rule.

Decision 2

Why file notes to a matter?

A lawyer thinks in cases, not meetings. Attaching every summary to its matter is what lets March's settlement talk connect to October's deposition prep.

Decision 3

Why hide the raw transcript?

The transcript is evidence; the summary is what the lawyer needs. Leading with the summary keeps less discoverable text in front of people.

Decision 4

Why only summarize what was said?

In litigation, an invented fact can lead to court sanctions. Grounding every point in the audio is what makes the AI usable at all.

12Looking back

What CaseNotes taught me.

Compliance is a design input

Most AI products add compliance after the design is done. In law it has to shape the first screen.

Defaults are decisions

Whether a call starts privileged or open is a product choice with legal weight. A wrong default creates a liability.

Design the system, then the screens

The matter-linked structure did more for lawyers than any single screen, because it made every other feature possible.
